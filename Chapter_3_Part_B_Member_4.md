## 3.4 Data Collection Procedures

The proposed research will collect data relevant to Data Loss Prevention (DLP) and insider-threat detection. Based on the group's Assignment 1 review, relevant data sources may include publicly available or synthetic datasets containing user activity, file access, file transfer, network activity, email/communication events, or cloud activity.

The data collection process will follow these steps:

1. Identify datasets that are relevant to DLP and insider-threat detection.
2. Check whether the selected dataset contains the attributes required by the research objectives.
3. Confirm that the dataset is publicly available, synthetic, or otherwise legally permitted for academic use.
4. Remove or avoid confidential and personally identifiable information.
5. Prepare and organise the data for analysis.
6. Apply suitable preprocessing, such as cleaning, formatting, feature selection, and handling missing values.
7. Separate data into suitable training, validation, and testing sets where applicable.
8. Document the dataset source, characteristics, limitations, and preprocessing steps.

The final dataset will be selected after the research objectives and methodology are confirmed.

## 3.5 Ethical and Legal Considerations

Ethical and legal requirements are important because DLP research can involve sensitive information and user behaviour.

The project will:

- Use only datasets that are publicly available, synthetic, or authorised for academic research.
- Avoid collecting unnecessary personal or confidential information.
- Follow applicable data-protection, privacy, copyright, and licensing requirements.
- Keep research data secure and restrict access to project members.
- Avoid exposing real organisational secrets or personally identifiable information.
- Report findings responsibly and only for academic/research purposes.
- Clearly acknowledge dataset and software sources.

If human participants are required in a later stage, appropriate institutional approval and informed consent will be obtained before data collection.

## 3.6 Proposed Evaluation Plan

The proposed DLP solution will be evaluated using suitable classification and security-performance measures. Depending on the final methodology, the evaluation may include:

- Accuracy
- Precision
- Recall
- F1-score
- Confusion matrix
- False-positive rate
- False-negative rate
- Processing time or latency, where relevant

The evaluation will compare the proposed approach with an appropriate baseline or alternative method where feasible. Particular attention will be given to the limitations identified in Assignment 1, including class imbalance, false positives/negatives, realistic-data limitations, real-time feasibility, privacy, and resistance to evasion.

No experimental numerical results should be presented at the proposal stage as if they were already obtained.

## 3.8 Expected Outcomes

The proposed research is expected to produce:

1. A structured DLP research framework addressing data leakage and insider-threat risks.
2. A suitable dataset or sample-input structure for evaluating the proposed approach.
3. Detection/classification outputs for normal and suspicious activities.
4. Quantitative evaluation using appropriate performance metrics.
5. An analysis of the strengths and limitations of the proposed approach.
6. Recommendations for improving DLP through behaviour-aware, privacy-conscious and practical detection/prevention methods.
7. A clearer understanding of challenges related to realistic data, multi-channel protection, real-time deployment and evasion resistance.

These are proposed outcomes and will be confirmed only after the research and evaluation are completed.
